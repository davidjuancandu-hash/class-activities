🌍 Unit 5 — THE PLASTIC AGE (pages 74–75)

Look at the cover in your textbook image: Many colorful plastic objects form a large circle against a black background. Think about how many plastic objects people use.

Important photo caption: “More than 500 pieces of plastic found in the digestive tract of a baby bird in the North Pacific Gyre.” A digestive tract is the part of the body that processes food. The North Pacific Gyre is the location named in the caption.

IN THIS UNIT, you will: Evaluate the amount of plastic in our lives; consider how plastic waste can be reduced; consider disadvantages to green solutions; present arguments for and against a green idea.

LISTENING: Recognize fact and opinion. A fact can be checked, such as a number or place stated in the caption. An opinion tells what a person thinks or believes.

SPEAKING: Present a balanced view. This means considering both advantages and disadvantages before making a judgment.

GRAMMAR: The unit names the passive voice as a future skill. An easy example to recognize is “Plastic is used in many products.” Here the focus is on the material, not the person using it. You do not need a full passive-voice lesson for this activity.

CRITICAL THINKING: Make a balanced judgment. Do not look at only the benefits or only the problems.

CONNECT TO THE TOPIC: The page asks: “What items can you identify?” and “How much plastic trash do you think you could find around your neighborhood in one hour?” The second question asks for an estimate or opinion, not a proven number.

Practice examples (added to help you understand the page): Using a reusable bottle could reduce disposable bottle use; a possible difficulty is remembering to bring it. These are possible arguments, not facts reported by the textbook page.

Directions: Answer one A/B/C/D question at a time. Read all four options. Use this guide whenever you need help. You may review and change earlier answers. There is no countdown. Complete all 24 missions, then FINISH → GRADE → DOWNLOAD RESULTS.

Your challenge: Read each question and every answer. Use the help guide and your grammar notes as often as you need. All questions have one best answer.

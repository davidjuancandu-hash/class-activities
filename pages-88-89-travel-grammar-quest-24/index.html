UNIT 5 • PAGES 88–89: WHY WE TRAVEL. Today's skills are choosing, combining, and editing because, since, and so; planning trips and reasons.

REASONS: because and since introduce why something happens. Example: Rafael visited Chichen Itza because he wanted to learn about Mayan culture.

RESULTS: so introduces what happens as a result. Example: Some travelers worry about climate change, so they travel by train or ship.

COMMAS: When a because/since clause comes first, add a comma after that whole clause: Since Chuy wants exercise, he is going hiking. When the because/since clause comes after the main clause, usually no comma is needed: Ana is staying on a Vietnamese farm because she's interested in Vietnam. When so joins two complete clauses, place a comma before it: Sonya loves cooking, so she's taking a food tour.

PAGE 88 EXAMPLES: Sonya: Italian food tour (loves cooking). Ana: Vietnamese farm (interested in Vietnam). Chuy: Peru hiking (wants exercise). Mona: Beijing weekend (likes old palaces). Other examples include Brazil/Portuguese, Mexico/Mayan culture, and France/cycling.

PAGE 89 FARM-STAY ARTICLE: An organic-farm stay in Spain can teach travelers about local culture, improve Spanish through daily contact with local people, and support physical health through exercise. Practice fixing commas around because and so.

PLAN & WRITE: In the textbook, students brainstorm two interesting trips and write a reason to go for each one. A trip is an activity or destination (e.g., a food tour of Italy); a reason is the purpose (e.g., to learn about cooking).
